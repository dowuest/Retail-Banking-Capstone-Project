4 Irreversible Trends
Structural — not cyclical
24%
NIB share (down from ~35% since 2021)
  • NIB collapse: permanently re-educated customers; any plan assuming >28% is wrong
  • Online banks set permanent rate floor: 1.5–2.5% in neutral rate environment
  • Side-account shift: customers parking savings at MMFs/neobanks without leaving primary bank — invisible in churn metrics, devastating to NIM
  • A bank can show flat deposit counts while losing its most profitable balances
  • By the time attrition appears in the P&L, the relationship has typically been deteriorating for 3–5 years
  • Post-SVB flight to quality: ~$300B flowed to GSIBs; durable competitive advantage

SMB + Affluent Pivot
The only scenario-robust strategy
4.5–5.5×
Target LTV/CAC ratio for mid-tier bank
  • Holds in all 3 scenarios SMB + affluent improves relative position in base case
  • Current mid-tier LTV/CAC: 2.0–2.5×; GSIB: 6.2× — the gap is the strategy
  • Every quarter of delay = relationship inception loss that cannot be recovered
  • Mid-tier currently in “no man's land”: insufficient digital to acquire efficiently; insufficient relationship depth to retain without paying market rates
  • An explicit archetype commitment is required — gradual improvement on both dimensions simultaneously achieves neither advantage

The Economics Gap

Mid-tier banks currently operate at $320–380 CAC with $650–850 5yr LTV — a 2.0–2.5× LTV/CAC ratio. GSIBs achieve 6.2×. The target state for a mid-tier bank executing the SMB + Affluent strategy over 3–4 years is 4.5–5.5×. Deposit beta is the primary NIM driver and it comes from relationship depth, not product pricing. The ~$900B CD maturity cycle (2025–26) is the nearest-term opportunity — with the 2025 tranche largely complete, the active window is 2026 maturities. Digital onboarding must be in place before the maturity date.
