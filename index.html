Executive Summary

US financial services is at a structural inflection point. Loans face a bifurcating landscape where credit cards remain the dominant profit engine (3.87% ROA, 3.5× industry average) while non-banks have captured mortgage origination and fintechs have displaced banks in personal lending entirely. Payments revenue pools of $150–170B face simultaneous compression: acquiring margins race toward zero, FedNow threatens wire fee income, and Big Tech builds durable payment moats. Deposits — at $18.3T total — are being structurally repriced as NIB share collapsed from 35% to 24% in 24 months, online banks set a permanent rate floor of 1.5–2.5%, and $7T in money market funds compete for rate-sensitive balances. The common thread: undifferentiated, mass-retail positioning is indefensible in all three lines. The institutions that will win are those that move up the value chain — into SMB, affluent, and relationship-depth strategies — before the window closes.

Cards dominate; non-banks own origination

  • Non-banks at 53% mortgage share; fintechs lead personal loans
$150B+ revenue at risk from 4 simultaneous disruptions
  • 5+ participant layers; interchange is largest fee pool

$18.3T market; GSIBs structurally advantaged post-SVB
  • $7T MMF pool competes directly for rate-sensitive deposits
  • Online banks set 1.5–2.5% permanent rate floor
Disruptive Forces
BNPL, AI underwriting, and interchange regulation reshape credit
  • BNPL at $109B GMV; Klarna AI cut 700 FTE equivalent
  • CCCA could reduce interchange revenue 50% if enacted
FedNow at $0.045/txn competes against $25 wire fees
  • 900+ FedNow institutions by Aug 2024; community banks 78%
  • Stablecoins legalized at institutional scale (OCC, Dec 2025)
"Side account" behavior is invisible in churn metrics but devastating to NIM
  • Customers not leaving — they're parking savings elsewhere
  • Open banking / FRB 1033 could cut switching cost to near zero
Economic Impact
JPMorgan's 32% ROE in cards is a structural outlier — not a benchmark
  • Top 6 banks earned $197B in consumer revenue (FY2024)
  • Goldman Marcus lost $3B+ exiting personal loans
Acquiring margin compressed to sub-20bps; only software-attached models survive
  • $148.5B in interchange fees in 2024 at ~2.35% average rate
  • FX spreads collapsing: Wise/Airwallex eliminate opacity in major corridors
Mid-tier cost of deposits at 1.8–2.4%; target is 1.2–1.6% via SMB/affluent pivot
  • LTV/CAC for mid-tier currently 2.0–2.5×; target 4.5–5.5×
  • $900B in 5%+ CDs maturing 2025–26: largest acquisition event of the cycle
Leadership Recommendation
Immediately commit capital to SMB + Affluent relationship depth across all three lines: capture the $900B CD maturity wall in deposits, anchor lending in cards and HELOCs where structural advantage exists, and invest in real-time payment infrastructure before FedNow makes the bank's wire fee income indefensible. Generic mass-retail positioning loses in every three-year scenario — and the competitive clock runs independently of the rate cycle.
